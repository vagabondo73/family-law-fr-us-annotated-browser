99.04 — Order of Delivery

in forceIn force since : 1977-12-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199990 · Rule 99 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

If the court finds that the facts stated in the affidavit show that the party has the right to immediate possession an order shall be issued directing the sheriff to take possession of the property and deliver it to the party upon compliance with Rule 99.06.

#### Committee Note – 1981

The source is prior Rule 99.02.

There is a change in that prior Rule 99.02 allowed the clerk to make the order and also provided that the filing of the affidavit required the order of delivery to be made while this rule provides that the court must make a finding concerning the facts stated in the affidavit before issuing the order.

See also : 99.06 (mo-rules), 99.02 (mo-rules)

State v. Holloway, 992 S.W.2d 886 (Mo. App. 1999)

Missouri Court of Appeals · 1999-05-03 · No. 22277 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals opinion applying or construing Rule 99.04 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Rule 99.04 provides that'once an Order of Delivery is issued by the court, the sheriff is directed “to take possession of the property.”
Secondly, Rule 99.04 (“Order of Delivery” in a replevin action) mandates that the sheriff do more than the merely serve a court order.

Union State Bank of Clinton v. Dolan, 718 S.W.2d 522 (Mo. App. W.D. 1986)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 1986-08-12 · No. WD 37548 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing Rule 99.04, Rule 99.06, Rule 99.11, Rule 85.07, Rule 85.04, Rule 85.11 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

There is no requirement that the court make specific finding of facts stated in the affidavit under Rule 99.04.
Under Rule 99.06, the purpose of the delivery bond is to prosecute the action without delay, for the return of the property to defendant, or in default thereof, to pay all damages for injuries to the property and for its taking and detention and for all costs.
If this were a jurisdictional matter, there would be no reason for Rule 99.11, the purpose of which is to protect a defendant with a sufficient replevin bond by a curative remedy.
