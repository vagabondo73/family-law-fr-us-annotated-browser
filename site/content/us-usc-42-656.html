42 U.S.C. § 656 — Support obligation as obligation to State; amount; discharge in bankruptcy

in forceIn force since : 1997-08-05 · Applicable : US

Official source : https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title42-section656&num=0&edition=prelim · govinfo (USCODE) · Interactive browser

Current text

(a) Collection processes

(1) The support rights assigned to the State pursuant to section 608(a)(3) of this title or secured on behalf of a child receiving foster care maintenance payments shall constitute an obligation owed to such State by the individual responsible for providing such support. Such obligation shall be deemed for collection purposes to be collectible under all applicable State and local processes.

(2) The amount of such obligation shall be-

(A) the amount specified in a court order which covers the assigned support rights, or

(B) if there is no court order, an amount determined by the State in accordance with a formula approved by the Secretary.

(3) Any amounts collected from a noncustodial parent under the plan shall reduce, dollar for dollar, the amount of his obligation under subparagraphs (A) and (B) of paragraph (2).

(b) Nondischargeability

A debt (as defined in section 101 of title 11) owed under State law to a State (as defined in such section) or municipality (as defined in such section) that is in the nature of support and that is enforceable under this part is not released by a discharge in bankruptcy under title 11.

Binding interpretations (1)

Jackson v. Rapps, 947 F.2d 332 (8th Cir. 1991)

United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit · 1991-10-17 · 90-2604, 90-2605 published
Basis (b): identical text — functional-review

Justification : The decision interpreted 42 U.S.C. § 656(a)(2) and § 656(a)(2)(B), requiring the obligation to be set by a support order or, absent one, by a State formula approved by the Secretary; that operative language appears verbatim in the current text. Although § 656(a)(2)(B) was later amended to change “Secretary, and” to “Secretary.”, the change was technical and did not alter the provision’s wording or function as applied in Jackson v. Rapps.

Summary (non-official) : The court held that Missouri’s policy of setting an AFDC reimbursement debt equal to all public assistance paid violated Title IV-D requirements. Under 42 U.S.C. §§ 654(13) and 656(a)(2), when no court order establishes support, the state must determine the obligation using a federally approved formula and the criteria required by federal regulations.

The amount of the obligation is set by a court order for support, unless there is no court order covering support rights. 42 U.S.C. § 656(a)(2). In that instance, the obligation shall be “determined by the State in accordance with a formula approved by the Secretary.” 42 U.S.C. § 656(a)(2)(B).
We are convinced that the Director’s policy in setting the amount of state debt as the total amount of public assistance paid out violates federal regulations governing the AFDC program. Here, statutes and regulations covering the AFDC program require that the states follow criteria set forth in 45 C.F.R. § 302.53(a). 42 U.S.C. § 654(13) (1988) provides: “... the State will comply with such other requirements and standards as the Secretary [of Health and Human Services] determines to be necessary to the establishment of an effective program for ... obtaining support orders, and collecting support payments.
