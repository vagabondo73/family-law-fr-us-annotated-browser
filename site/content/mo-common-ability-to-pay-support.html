ability-to-pay-support — Ability to pay child support — earning capacity and resources

in forceIn force since : 2025-03-04 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/file.jsp?id=218056 · Leading source: Melissa M. Carter v. Drew B. Carter, No. ED112335 (Mo. App. E.D. 2025) · Leading source: Selleck v. Summary rule

“In determining a parent’s ability to pay child support, a court may consider past, present, and anticipated earning capacity.” (Carter, 2025). “While a party’s salary is usually the most significant component of a parent’s ability to pay child support, the court may consider other resources” (Selleck v. Selleck, 2017). “Instead, in determining ability to pay, the focus is on whether the individual after making required child support payments can meet his remaining needs and obligations.” (Kropf v. Jones, 2015).

In determining a parent’s ability to pay child support, a court may consider past, present, and anticipated earning capacity.
Missouri recognizes the concept of transmutation which turns non-marital property into marital property as a consequence of a transfer of that property, either as a gift or by express or implied agreement of the spouses.
Under Rule 84.04(e), a brief must include an argument section that discusses the points relied on.
Section 452.330.1 requires the trial court to set apart to each spouse any non-marital property and to “divide the marital property and marital debts in such proportions as the court deems just. . . .” The trial court has broad discretion in dividing marital property.
