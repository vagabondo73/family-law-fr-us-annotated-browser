emancipation-burden — Emancipation — burden of proof

in forceIn force since : 2015-06-02 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courtlistener.com/c/S.W.3d/462/748/ · Leading source: Fugate ex rel. Stroup v. Fugate, 582 S.W.2d 663 (Mo. banc 1979) · Leading source: Kay v. Keller, 462 S.W.3d 748 (Mo. App. W.D. 2015) · Interactive browser

Summary rule

The party asserting emancipation bears the burden: “Under Wurth plaintiff has the burden of proving that she is emancipated and under the evidence presented at the hearing she cannot sustain that burden.” (Fugate ex rel. Stroup v. Fugate, Mo. banc 1979); applied in Kay v. Keller (2015), where the father argued “Mother failed to meet her burden of proving emancipation.”

Rule sources : Fugate ex rel. Stroup v. Fugate, 582 S.W.2d 663 (Mo. banc 1979), Kay v. Keller, 462 S.W.3d 748 (Mo. App. W.D. 2015)

Kay v. Keller, 462 S.W.3d 748 (Mo. App. W.D. 2015)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 2015-06-02 · WD 78235 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing § 452.340, 74.04, 55.28 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

Fourth, Father argues that the circuit court erred in finding Son emancipated because Mother failed to meet her burden of proving emancipation.
Father argues that Mother’s contention in support of her summary judgment motion, that the custodial parent must bring the action for continued support pursuant to Section 452.340.4 before the child’s eighteenth birthday, is not supported by law.
First, we are not confident that the evidence presented at the June 6, 2014, hearing is even relevant to our consideration of whether Mother was entitled to judgment as a matter of law pursuant to Rule 74.04(c).
We discussed that our Eastern District has permitted trial courts to hear oral testimony on a motion for summary judgment based on Rule 55.28, but expressed our reluctance to agree that Rule 55.28 is applicable to such motions because Rule 55.28 conditions the trial court’s use of oral testimony on deciding motions that are “based on facts not appearing of record.”
