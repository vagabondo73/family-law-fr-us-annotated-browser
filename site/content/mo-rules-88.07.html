88.07 — Termination of Mediation

in forceIn force since : 1991-07-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199863 · Rule 88 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

(a) At any time after two hours of mediation either party may terminate mediation ordered under Rule 88.04.

(b) The mediator shall terminate mediation whenever the mediator believes (1) that continuation of the process would harm or prejudice one or more of the parties or the children or (2) that the ability or willingness of any party to participate meaningfully in mediation is so lacking that a reasonable agreement is unlikely. (c) The mediator shall report the termination of mediation to the court. The mediator shall not state the reason for termination except when the termination is due to a conflict of interest or bias on the part of the mediator, in which case another mediator may be appointed.

art. 1535-4 · Légifrance88.07partial—The conciliator or mediator must inform the judge of difficulties and of the success or failure of the process. Rule 88.07(c) only requires the mediator to report termination to the court; it imposes no general duty to report difficulties or the outcome.
art. 1535-5 · Légifrance88.07partial—The judge may terminate conciliation or mediation at a party’s or mediator’s request, or on the judge’s own initiative when the process is compromised or pointless, with the case then resuming. Rule 88.07 permits termination by the parties or mediator under specified conditions and requires court notification, but lacks the CPC’s general judicial termination and resumption provisions.

The Missouri provisions govern termination of mediation, but do not provide any effect on an action’s expiration period.

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing Rule 88.07 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Section (a) of Rule 88.07, entitled “Termination of Mediation,” provides that a party may terminate mediation ordered under Rule 88.04 at any time after two hours.
It is apparent that Rule 88.07 contemplates the constitutionality issues involved with delaying access to the courts that were presented in Gaertner, because it provides a measure to avoid such unconstitutionality.
