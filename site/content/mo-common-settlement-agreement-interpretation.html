settlement-agreement-interpretation — Contract interpretation applied to separation / marital settlement agreements incorporated in decrees

in forceIn force since : 2014-06-03 · Applicable : MO

Summary rule

“A separation agreement is a contract.” (Boden v. Boden, 2007). “The terms of the separation agreement, incorporated into the 1990 dissolution decree, are construed according to the principles governing contract interpretation.” “The cardinal rule in the interpretation of a contract is to ascertain the intention of the parties and to give effect to that intention.” (Roberts v. Roberts, 2014). Likewise, “marital settlement agreements are construed using ordinary contract principles.” (LeKander v. Estate of LeKander, 2011).

Rule sources : Roberts v. Roberts, 432 S.W.3d 789 (Mo. App. W.D. 2014), Boden v. Boden, 229 S.W.3d 169 (Mo. App. E.D. 2007), Lekander v. Estate of Lekander, 345 S.W.3d 282 (Mo. App. S.D. 2011)

Roberts v. Roberts, 432 S.W.3d 789 (Mo. App. W.D. 2014)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 2014-06-03 · No. WD 76679 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing § 452.330, § 452.830, § 452.325 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

The terms of the separation agreement, incorporated into the 1990 dissolution decree, are construed according to the principles governing contract interpretation.
The cardinal rule in the interpretation of a contract is to ascertain the intention of the parties and to give effect to that intention.
Analysis Section 452.330.5 provides: The court’s order as it affects distribution of marital property shall be a final order not subject to modification; provided, however, that orders intended to be qualified domestic relations orders affecting pension, profit sharing and stock bonus plans pursuant to the U.S. Internal Revenue Code shall be modifiable only for the purpose of establishing or maintaining the order as a qualified domestic relations order or to revise or conform its terms so as to effectuate the expressed intent of the order.
“It is well-settled in Missouri that only the portion of retirement benefits which are acquired during marriage are considered marital property subject to division under § 452.830.”

Boden v. Boden, 229 S.W.3d 169 (Mo. App. E.D. 2007)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2007-05-15 · Nos. ED 87900, ED 88198 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing 84.14, § 452.325 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

A separation agreement is a contract.
“The cardinal rule in the interpretation of a contract is to ascertain the intention of the parties and to give effect to that intention.”
We render such judgment as the court ought to give pursuant to Rule 84.14 and resolve the ambiguity in the parties’ maintenance provision against the wife.
The trial court had no authority under the parties’ agreement, the decree of legal separation, or section 452.325 to modify the maintenance terms of the parties’ agreement.
