41.08 — Signatures of Judicial Officers and Court Officials

in forceIn force since : 2026-01-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199556 · Rule 41 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Order dated 2025-03-04 (Missouri Bar copy) · Interactive browser

Current text

(a) Signature of judicial officer. Documents requiring a judge's or commissioner's signature may be signed by an original signature, stamped signature, or an electronic graphic representation of a signature. When the signature appears on a docket sheet entry, the entry may be signed in the following manner: /s/ John or Jane Judge.

(b) Signature of clerk. Documents requiring a clerk's signature may be signed by an original signature, stamped signature, an electronic graphic representation of a signature, or /s/ John or Jane Clerk. (c) Signature of court reporter and transcriber. Documents requiring a court reporter's or transcriber's signature may be signed by an original signature, stamped signature, an electronic graphic representation of a signature, or /s/ John or Jane Person.

Binding interpretations (1)

Amoso Realty, LLC v. Milton, 513 S.W.3d 373 (Mo. App. E.D. 2016)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2016-12-27 · No. ED 104375 published
Basis (b): identical text — text-identical

Justification : The opinion (2016-12-27) predates the current version of Rule 41.08 (in force since 2026-01-01) but quotes the language it applies — “Documents requiring a judge’s or commissioner’s signature may be signed by an original signature, stamped signature, or an electronic graphic representation of a signature.” — and that language appears verbatim (normalized for punctuation/case) in the current text; the interpreted language is therefore identical in wording and function.

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing Rule 67.02, § 512.020 RSMo, Rule 74.01, Rule 41.08 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

The trial court reasoned that Amoso and Milton’s Stipulation of Dismissal was a voluntary dismissal under Rule 67.02(a), which was effective—without judicial approval—on the date It was filed. Thus, the trial court found that it lacked jurisdiction and denied Weathers’s motion to intervene.
Id. Section 512.020 provides the general mechanism for determining who may appeal in a civil case, it states: Any party to a suit aggrieved by any judgment of any trial court in any civil cause from which an appeal is not prohibited by the constitution, nor clearly limited in special statutory proceedings, may take his or her appeal to a court having appellate jurisdiction from any: ... (5) Final judgment in the case or from any special order after final judgment in the cause; .... (Emphasis added.) A final judgment is a prerequisite to appellate review, subject to exceptions not relevant here.
Id. We further explained that the docket entry did not constitute a “judgment” because it lacked another requirement of Rule 74.01(a): it was not signed or initialed by the judge.
