474.070 — Legitimation by marriage.

in forceIn force since : 1955-08-28 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=474.070 · Interactive browser

Current text

If a man, having by a woman a child or children, afterward intermarries with her and recognizes the child or children to be his, they are thereby legitimated.

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing § 211.442, § 474.070 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

Section 211.442 provides as follows: “As used in sections 211.442 to 211.492 ‘parent’ means a biological parent or parents of a child, as well as the husband of a natural mother at the time the child was conceived, or a parent or parents of child by adoption, and includes both the mother and putative father of an illegitimate child.
See also Section 474.070, Probate Code — Intestate Succession and Wills, which provides for legitimation of an illegitimate child.
The father of an illegitimate child shall have no legal relationship unless he, prior to the entry of a decree under sections 211.442 to 211.492 has acknowledged the child as his own by affirmatively asserting his paternity; provided, however, that a parent whose identity is known and who can be personally served shall be served by process as provided in chapter 506, RSMo. of this state and made a party to any action under sections 211.442 to 211.492.”
While the first sentence of Sec. 211.442 defines “parent” to include a biological parent and both the putative father and the mother of an illegitimate child, the definition is modified by the second sentence of that section which recites that the putative father does not acquire a legal relationship to his illegitimate child unless he acknowledges the child “by affirmatively asserting his paternity.”

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing § 474.070, 52.04 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

If the proof of legitimacy is established pursuant to § 474.070 we believe the child becomes a “child of the marriage” regardless of when he/she was born.
Relying on Rule 52.04(a)(2) we said: It can hardly be disputed that a presumptive father has an “interest relating to the subject of the action” when the action concerns a young child who was born to his wife during their marriage, and who, according to the uncontradicted statements at oral argument, carries his name and has been supported by him.
It is clear that a child born during one marriage can be legitimated by the husband of a second marriage by virtue of § 474.070, regardless of the presumption that a child born during a marriage is the legitimate child of that marriage.
