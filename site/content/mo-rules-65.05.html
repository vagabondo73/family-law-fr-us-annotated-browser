65.05 — Affidavit for Continuance – Amendment – Admission of Facts Set Forth

in forceIn force since : 1973-09-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199701 · Rule 65 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

If the court shall be of the opinion that the affidavit is insufficient, it shall permit it to be amended. If, after such amendment, the affidavit does not contain a sufficient statement of facts, the court shall overrule it. If the court finds the affidavit sufficient, the civil action shall be continued unless the opposing party will admit that the witness, if present, would swear to the facts set out in the affidavit, in which event the civil action shall not be continued. The party moving for the continuance may read as the evidence of such witness the facts stated in the affidavit. The opposing party may disprove the facts disclosed, or prove any contradictory statements made by the absent witness, or otherwise impeach the credibility of the absent witness.

Binding interpretations (2)

Seabaugh v. Milde Farms, Inc., 816 S.W.2d 202 (Mo. 1991)

Supreme Court of Missouri · 1991-09-10 · No. 73372 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Supreme Court of Missouri opinion applying or construing Rule 65.05, Rule 66.02 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

This assumption is based on the language of Rule 65.05, providing: “If the court finds the affidavit sufficient, the civil action shall be continued unless the opposing party will admit that the witness, if present, would swear to the facts set out in the affidavit....” As a general principle, the grant or denial of a continuance is largely within the discretion of the trial court.
Rule 66.02 provides, “The court, in furtherance of convenience or to avoid prejudice, or when separate trials will be conducive to expedition and economy, may order a separate trial of any claim....” In the case before the Court, the claims of the Seabaugh children and the claims of Linda and Dennis Seabaugh are completely separate claims.
No authority cited or found holds that a trial court is limited to considering only the allegations found within the four corners of the motion when deciding whether to grant or deny a continuance under Rules 65.04 and 65.05.
