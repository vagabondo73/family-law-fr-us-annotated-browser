commingling-tracing — Commingling and tracing of separate property

in forceIn force since : 2016-02-05 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courtlistener.com/c/S.W.3d/481/855/ · Leading source: Andrews v. Andrews, 289 S.W.3d 717 (Mo. App. E.D. 2009) · Leading source: Keet v. Bell, 481 S.W.3d 855 (Mo. App. S.D. 2016) · Leading source: Bold v. Bold, 912 S.W.2d 477 (Mo. banc 1995) · Interactive browser

Summary rule

“In addition, section 452.330.4 specifically states that nonmarital property does not become marital property solely because it is commingled with marital property.” “Instead, onee commingling occurs, the party claiming a separate property interest must establish by clear and convincing evidence that an identifiable portion can be traced to specific separate property.” (Andrews v. Andrews, 2009; the reporter text reads “onee” [sic] for “once”). “Once commingling occurs, the party claiming separate property has the burden of establishing that an identifiable portion of the funds can be traced to specific nonmarital assets.” (Keet v. Bell, 2016). “It was husband’s burden to trace the non-marital property into his capital contributions” (Bold v. Bold, Mo. banc 1995).

Rule sources : Andrews v. Andrews, 289 S.W.3d 717 (Mo. App. E.D. 2009), Keet v. Bell, 481 S.W.3d 855 (Mo. App. S.D. 2016), Bold v. Bold, 912 S.W.2d 477 (Mo. banc 1995)

Andrews v. Andrews, 289 S.W.3d 717 (Mo. App. E.D. 2009)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2009-05-12 · No. ED 90797 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing § 452.330, § 452.355, 84.14 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

In addition, section 452.330.4 specifically states that nonmarital property does not become marital property solely because it is commingled with marital property.
Instead, onee commingling occurs, the party claiming a separate property interest must establish by clear and convincing evidence that an identifiable portion can be traced to specific separate property.
Pursuant to section 452.330 RSMo (2000) , the trial court "shall set apart to each spouse such spouse's nonmarital property," and shall divide the remaining marital property as it deems just after consideration of the relevant factors.
Section 452.355.1 allows the court to order a party to pay attorney's fees after "considering all relevant factors including the financial resources of both parties, the merits of the case and the actions of the parties during the pendency of the action...." The trial court is vested with broad discretion in this regard, and we will reverse the award of attorney's fees only if the court has abused its discretion.
