77.06 — Execution for Costs

in forceIn force since : 1981-01-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=200285 · Interactive browser

Current text

An execution may issue for costs either before or after final judgment.

Binding interpretations (2)

Sadowski v. Brewer, 693 S.W.2d 891 (Mo. App. S.D. 1985)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District · 1985-06-27 · No. 13764 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District opinion applying or construing 77.01, 77.06 (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

Rule 77.01 states: “In civil actions, the party prevailing shall recover his costs against the other party, unless otherwise provided in these rules or by law.”
It is substantially the same as prior Rule 77.06, which provided: “In all civil actions, or proceedings of any kind, the party prevailing shall recover his costs against the other party, except in those cases in which a different provision is made by law.”
Alternatively, plaintiffs contend that if the taxing of costs against defendant was not mandatory under Rule 77.01, the trial court nonetheless abused its discretion in taxing the costs against them inasmuch as this suit was necessitated by defendant’s arbitrary refusal to let them see Missy.
Prior Rule 77.06 appeared in the above form as far back as 1959.

State v. Cox, 639 S.W.2d 425 (Mo. App. W.D. 1982)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 1982-09-07 · No. WD 32701 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing Rule 77.06 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

See specifically, Automagic Vendors, Inc. v. Morris, 386 S.W.2d 897 (Mo.1965) (en banc) (because there must be a specific provision authorizing costs to be taxed against the state, Civil Rule 77.06 which provided only that “the party prevailing shall recover his costs against the other party ...” was not adequate specifically to impose liability); and Hartwig-Dischinger Realty Co. v. Unemployment Compensation Commission, 350 Mo. 690, 168 S.W.2d 78 (1943) (en banc) (awarding costs against the Unemployment Compensation Commission was in error because the commission was “merely representing the state” in the case). . This case is another example of the problems which result when the state attorney general’s office is not in control from its inception of all litigation involving the state.
