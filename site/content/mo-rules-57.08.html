57.08 — Depositions for Use in Foreign Jurisdictions

in forceIn force since : 1975-01-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199677 · Rule 57 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

Whenever the deposition of any person is to be taken in this state pursuant to the laws of another state or of the United States or of another country for use in proceedings there, the circuit court in the county where the deponent is found may, upon

*ex parte*application, make an order directing issuance of a subpoena as provided in Rule 57.09, in aid of the taking of the deposition, and having due regard for the laws and rules of such foreign jurisdiction, may make such orders as could be made if the deposition were intended for use in this jurisdiction.

State ex rel. Jones v. Holden, 384 S.W.3d 700 (Mo. App. S.D. 2011)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District · 2011-05-18 · No. SD 31208 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District opinion applying or construing Rule 57.08, Rule 53.01, Rule 55.01 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Rule 57.08 does not provide that an application under its provisions constitutes or must be in the form of an original petition, nor does the Rule suggest that such an application requires service of summons in accordance with Rule 54.
Discussion and Decision Rule 53.01 provides that “[a] civil action is commenced by filing a petition with the court.”
Rule 57.08 also does not indicate that an answer or other responsive pleading is permitted or required in accordance with Rule 55.01.

Central Electric Power Cooperative v. Sharp, 758 S.W.2d 483 (Mo. App. W.D. 1988)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 1988-10-04 · No. WD 40159 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing Rule 57.08 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Rule 57.07(d)(4) states: Errors and irregularities in the manner in which the testimony is transcribed or the deposition is prepared, signed, certified, sealed, endorsed, transmitted, filed, or otherwise dealt with by the officer under Rules 57.08 and 57.04 are waived unless a motion to suppress the depositions or some part thereof is made with reasonable promptness after such defect is, or with due diligence might have been, ascertained. Clearly, the complaints raised by Central Electric were waived because no motion to suppress the deposition was filed with reasonable promptness after the defects to which objection was made might have been ascertained. Central Electric knew that it had taken the deposition of McCauley.
