gal-role — Guardian ad litem in custody proceedings — role

in forceIn force since : 1999-11-09 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courtlistener.com/c/S.W.3d/7/401/ · Leading source: Rombach v. Rombach, 867 S.W.2d 500 (Mo. banc 1993) · Leading source: Mund v. Mund, 7 S.W.3d 401 (Mo. banc 1999) · Interactive browser

Summary rule

“Mandatory appointment of a guardian ad litem is not intended to benefit either of the parties in a dissolution proceeding.” (Rombach v. Rombach, Mo. banc 1993). “Alleged inaction of a guardian ad litem, if any, is not tantamount to a claim of trial court error.” (Mund v. Mund, Mo. banc 1999).

Rule sources : Rombach v. Rombach, 867 S.W.2d 500 (Mo. banc 1993), Mund v. Mund, 7 S.W.3d 401 (Mo. banc 1999)

Mund v. Mund, 7 S.W.3d 401 (Mo. banc 1999)

Supreme Court of Missouri · 1999-11-09 · No. 81631 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Supreme Court of Missouri opinion applying or construing § 452.400, § 452.423 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

Alleged inaction of a guardian ad litem, if any, is not tantamount to a claim of trial court error.
The Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District, granted mother’s application to transfer the cause to this Court to decide the following question of general interest and importance: whether the trial court erred in failing to make findings of fact as required by sections 452.375.12 and 452.400.1, RSMo Supp.1997, and findings of fact and conclusions of law as required by section 452.375.2(5).
Section 452.423 authorizes the guardian ad litem to offer evidence but does not require the guardian ad litem to do so.
Section 452.400 states in pertinent part that, in considering whether to grant visitation to a spouse found to have committed domestic violence, the court shall consider “the parent’s history of inflicting, or tendency to inflict, physical harm, bodily injury, assault, or the fear of physical harm, bodily injury, or assault on other persons.”
