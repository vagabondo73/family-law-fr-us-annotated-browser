55.14 — Partnership Deemed Confessed, Unless Denied

in forceIn force since : 1973-09-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199645 · Rule 55 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

When parties sue or are sued as a partnership, and the names of the partners are set forth in the petition or counterclaim, the existence of the partnership shall be deemed confessed unless it be denied by specific negative averment, which shall include such supporting particulars as are peculiarly within the pleader's knowledge.

Wilbur Waggoner Equipment Rental & Excavating Co. v. Bumiller, 542 S.W.2d 32 (Mo. App. 1976)

Missouri Court of Appeals · 1976-08-03 · Nos. 36548 and 37235 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals opinion applying or construing Rule 55.14, § 509.150 RSMo (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

(Emphasis added.) It is the purpose of Rule 55.14 to save parties the difficult and often expensive process of proving the existence of a partnership when that issue is not contested. This purpose extends equally to cross-claims and third-party claims as to original petitions and counter-claims.
(Emphasis added.) Rule 55.14 (formerly 55.16) derived from § 509.150, RSMo.1969, provides specifically in the case of partnership that: When parties sue or are sued as a partnership, and the names of the partners are set forth in the petition or counterclaim, the existence of the partnership shall be deemed confessed unless it be denied by specific negative averment, which shall include such supporting particulars as are peculiarly within the pleader’s knowledge.
We conclude Rule 55.14 does apply to allegations of Maryland’s and Bumiller’s cross-claim and the clear allegation of partnership in Maryland’s pleadings to which no specific denial was made, becomes proof in the case, available for all purposes since, as a general rule, “[a]n admission on the pleadings is an admission for all the purposes of the cause.” 71 C.J.S. Pleadings § 160, p. 335, sufficient to support the trial court’s finding and conclusion.
