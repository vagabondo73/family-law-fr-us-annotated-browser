99.03 — Affidavit to Obtain Immediate Possesion of Property

in forceIn force since : 1977-12-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199989 · Rule 99 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

When a party requests immediate possession of personal property an affidavit shall be filed stating:

(a) The description of the property;

(b) Facts showing the party is entitled to the possession of the property;

(c) The actual value of the property;

(d) The property has not been seized under any legal process;

(e) The party is in danger of losing the property unless immediate possession is obtained or the property is otherwise secured.

#### Committee Note – 1981

The source is prior Rule 99.02.

Paragraph (b) requiring facts to be stated showing that the party is entitled to the possession of the property is new.

See also : 99.02 (mo-rules)

Marston v. Rose-Elash, 720 S.W.2d 783 (Mo. App. W.D. 1986)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 1986-12-09 · No. WD 37862 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing Rule 99.03, Rule 99.13, Rule 99.15 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

The dispositive question on appeal is whether in a replevin action not involving a pre-judgment seizure (Rule 99.03-.12), a Missouri court obtains jurisdiction through personal service when the property for which possession is sought is not in the state.
“... the value of the property shall be determined and damages for the taking, detention or injury may be assessed. The judgment shall be against the party ... for the return of the property or the value of the property, at the election of the party entitled to possession, and for damages assessed_” Under Rule 99.13, the prevailing party need not elect between return of the property or its assessed value, “... until the property is in the possession of the sheriff....” Rule 99.15 allows execution to issue, “ to the sheriff of the county where the property is located directing him to deliver the property to the party entitled thereto.”
Rule 99.15 tracks the venue statute for attachment and replevin suits, Section 508.-020 RSMo 1978; both require these cases be brought in the county where the property is found.

As to the valuation of the property, the purpose of the Rule 99.03(c) requirement that the actual value of the property be stated in the affidavit is for the court to determine if the amount of the replevin bond is sufficient.
Here, Green Hills alleged in its petition a description of and value of the property sought to be replev-ined as required by Rule 99.03, and as noted, bond was filed in an amount double the stated value of the property.

State ex rel. Tallen v. Marsh, 633 S.W.2d 458 (Mo. App. W.D. 1982)

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing Rule 99.03 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

We agree with relator that Supreme Court Rule 99.03 requires something more than such a conclusory statement.
We must then, to reach the meaning of Rule 99.03, take up the constitutional requirements for a prejudgment removal of property from defendant’s possession.
We conclude that an affidavit alleging evidentiary facts is required by Supreme Court Rule 99.03, construed as we have construed it to comply with due process standards of Mitchell.

John Deere Co. v. Sanders, 617 S.W.2d 606 (Mo. App. 1981)

Missouri Court of Appeals · 1981-05-14 · No. 11712 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals opinion applying or construing Rule 99.01, Rule 99.03 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Rule 99.01, provides that a person claiming the right to possession of personal property may bring an action in replevin for possession of the property.
Rule 99.03 provides that: “[w]hen a party requests immediate possession of personal property an affidavit shall be filed stating: a) The description of the property; b) Facts showing the party is entitled to the possession of the property; c) The actual value of the property; d) The property has not been seized under any legal process; e) The party is in danger of losing the property unless immediate possession is obtained or the property is otherwise secured.” Section 533.010, reads basically the same as Rule 99.03.
Applying this test, the petition alleges facts that meet the requirements of subpara-graphs a), c), d), and e) of Rule 99.03.
