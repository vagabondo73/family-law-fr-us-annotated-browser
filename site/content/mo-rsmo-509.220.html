509.220 — Private statutes, averments — laws of other states — judicial notice.

in forceIn force since : 1943-08-28 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=509.220 · Interactive browser

Current text

1. In pleading a private statute or a right derived therefrom, it shall be sufficient to refer to such statute by its title and the place where found in the session acts or in the revised statutes, and the court shall thereupon take judicial notice thereof.

2. In every action or proceeding wherein the pleading states that the law of another state is relied upon, the courts of this state shall take judicial notice of the public statutes and judicial decisions of said state.

Anderson’s motion to quash the judgment in the Missouri court was filed pursuant to Rule 74.06(b), which allows a Missouri court to relieve a party from, inter aha, a “void” judgment.
We take judicial notice of the statutes and case law of a sister state where the pleadings indicate that jurisdiction’s law must be applied. Sections 490.080, 509.220.2; Rule 55.21(b); Robinson v. Gaines, 331 S.W.2d 653, 655 (Mo.1960); Schumacher at 45.
However, Bank failed to preserve this argument on appeal. Rule 84.04(e) clearly states that the argument section of an appellant’s brief “shall be limited to those errors included in the ‘Points Relied On.’ ” Bank failed to raise the legal principle delineated in the preceding paragraph in its points relied on.

Robinson v. Gaines, 331 S.W.2d 653 (Mo. 1960)

Supreme Court of Missouri · 1960-02-08 · No. 47361 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Supreme Court of Missouri opinion applying or construing § 509.220 RSMo, § 509.090 RSMo (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

We now take judicial notice of the public statutes and judicial decisions of a sister state when a pleading “relies upon the law of another state or contains allegations which show that the law of another state must be applied.” Supreme Court Rule 3.14, 42 V.A.M.S.; Sections 509.220 (see Laws 1927, p. 156), 490.080 (see Laws 1949, p. 318, § 2) ; Bartling v. Firestone Tire & Rubber Co., Mo.App., 275 S.W.2d 618, 622 [1,2], and cases cited. (Statutory references are to RSMo 1949 and V.A.M.S., unless otherwise indicated.) In Missouri contributory negligence is an affirmative defense to be pleaded (section 509.090), and defendant has the burden of proving contributory negligence.
