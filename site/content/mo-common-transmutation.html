transmutation — Transmutation of separate property into marital property

in forceIn force since : 2025-03-04 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/file.jsp?id=218056 · Leading source: Melissa M. Carter v. Drew B. Carter, No. ED112335 (Mo. App. E.D. 2025) · Leading source: Virginia G. Eckelkamp v. Gary L. Eckelkamp, No. ED108437 (Mo. App. E.D. 2020) · Interactive browser

Summary rule

“Missouri recognizes the concept of transmutation which turns non-marital property into marital property as a consequence of a transfer of that property, either as a gift or by express or implied agreement of the spouses.” (Carter v. Carter, 2025). “[P]lacing separate property into the joint names of both spouses creates a presumption that the property has been transmuted into marital property.” (Eckelkamp v. Eckelkamp, 2020).

Rule sources : Melissa M. Carter v. Drew B. Carter, No. ED112335 (Mo. App. E.D. 2025), Virginia G. Eckelkamp v. Gary L. Eckelkamp, No. ED108437 (Mo. App. E.D. 2020)

Melissa M. Carter v. Drew B. Carter, No. ED112335 (Mo. App. E.D. 2025)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2025-03-04 · ED112335 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing 84.04, § 452.330 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

In determining a parent’s ability to pay child support, a court may consider past, present, and anticipated earning capacity.
Missouri recognizes the concept of transmutation which turns non-marital property into marital property as a consequence of a transfer of that property, either as a gift or by express or implied agreement of the spouses.
Under Rule 84.04(e), a brief must include an argument section that discusses the points relied on.
Section 452.330.1 requires the trial court to set apart to each spouse any non-marital property and to “divide the marital property and marital debts in such proportions as the court deems just. . . .” The trial court has broad discretion in dividing marital property.
