comity-foreign-judgments — Recognition of foreign-country divorce, custody and support judgments — comity

in forceIn force since : 2005-10-25 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courtlistener.com/c/S.W.3d/174/53/ · Leading source: Manor v. Manor, 811 S.W.2d 497 (Mo. App. E.D. 1991) · Leading source: T.H. v. A.S., 938 S.W.2d 910 (Mo. App. W.D. 1997) · Leading source: Welch v. Contreras, 174 S.W.3d 53 (Mo. App. W.D. 2005) · Interactive browser

Summary rule

“Based on the doctrine of comity, state and federal courts may give recognition and force and effect to judgments obtained in foreign countries to the same extent as in the case of judgments of sister states.” “A divorce decree obtained in a foreign country or a sister state carries a rebuttable presumption of validity.” (Manor v. Manor, 1991, Israeli support judgment). “In contrast to the full faith and credit clause of the federal constitution, which imposes obligations on the courts of a sister state, the doctrine of comity is a rule of voluntary consent” and “to apply the doctrine, the court must have the law of the foreign country properly before it.” (T.H. v. A.S., 1997). “Comity and the duty to recognize the validity and effect of court proceedings in a foreign jurisdiction are not regarded as absolute rights, but are rather self-imposed restraints.” (Welch v. Contreras, 2005).

T.H. v. A.S., 938 S.W.2d 910 (Mo. App. W.D. 1997)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 1997-01-07 · No. WD 50666 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing § 452.423 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

In contrast to the full faith and credit clause of the federal constitution, which imposes obligations on the courts of a sister state, the doctrine of comity is a rule of voluntary consent; it has been defined as a courtesy or a willingness to grant a privilege, not as a matter of right but out of deference, respect, and good will.
Although the doctrine of comity may establish a general principle allowing for the recognition of the laws and judicial decisions of a foreign country, to apply the doctrine, the court must have the law of the foreign country properly before it.
Section 452.423.2(2) provides that, if appointed in any proceeding for child custody, the guardian ad litem shall “conduct all necessary interviews with persons having contact with or knowledge of the child in order to ascertain the child’s wishes, feelings, attachments and attitudes,” and that, “[i]f appropriate, the child shall be interviewed.”
