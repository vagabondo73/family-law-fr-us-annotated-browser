97.04 — Grant of Preliminary Order

in forceIn force since : 1981-01-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199975 · Rule 97 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

If the court is of the opinion that the preliminary order in prohibition should be granted, such order shall be issued.

#### Committee Note – 1981

The source is prior Rule 97.02.

See also : 97.02 (mo-rules)

State ex rel. Vicker's, Inc. v. Teel, 806 S.W.2d 113 (Mo. App. 1991)

Missouri Court of Appeals · 1991-03-26 · No. 17238 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals opinion applying or construing Rule 97.04, Rule 74.03 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

A preliminary order in prohibition was entered. Rule 97.04.
Respondent correctly points out that the record before this court does not disclose that the clerk served notice upon plaintiff “of the entry by mail in the manner provided for in Rule 43.01” whereby the trial court dismissed case number CV186-630CC. The purpose of Rule 74.03, however, is to afford parties time in which to challenge judgments or orders of which they were otherwise unaware.
Respondent’s complaint that he did not receive the notice required by Rule 74.03 and that he was thereby denied procedural due process is denied. Respondent’s final complaint is that there was no “final judgment” in case number CV186-630CC because the April 3, 1990, docket entry “was never transmogrified into a final decree.”
