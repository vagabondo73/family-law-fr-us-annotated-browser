511.620 — Refusal of party to satisfy — proceedings.

in forceIn force since : 1939-08-28 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=511.620 · Interactive browser

Current text

If a person receiving satisfaction of a judgment or decree shall refuse within a reasonable time after request of the party interested therein to acknowledge satisfaction on the record, or cause the same to be done in the manner herein provided, the person so interested may, on notice given, apply to the court to have the same done, and the court may thereupon order satisfaction to be entered by the clerk, with like effect as if acknowledged as aforesaid; and the cost attending such acknowledgment shall be recovered of the party refusing by fee-bill, as in other cases.

J & M Securities v. Mees, 519 S.W.3d 465 (Mo. App. E.D. 2017)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2017-03-14 · No. ED 104680 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing § 516.350 RSMo, § 511.620 RSMo (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Under § 516.350.1, “judgments are conclusively presumed paid ten years after they were originally rendered unless a party has revived the judgment or entered a payment upon the record.”
Rule 74.09(c). .J&M cannot rely on its filed memorandum of Acknowledgment of Credit Toward Judgment on the Record to refute Appellant’s claims because that filing does not indicate with certainty that a payment and not a credit was made, nor is it probative of who made the payment or other credit. , Furthermore, § 511.620 applies “[i]f a person receiving satisfaction of a judgment or decree shall refuse within a reasonable time after request of the party interested therein to acknowledge satisfaction on the record[.]” Appellant stated in his affidavit filed with his motions that he had not had any contact with J&M since the entry of default judgment on May 5, 2005.
In response, J&M alleged a payment was made, sufficient to extend the judgment under § 516.350.1, because J&M filed an Acknowledgment of Credit Toward Judgment on the Record and submitted an account ledger demonstrating a $50.00 credit was applied to Appellant’s debt.

Wince v. McGarrah, 972 S.W.2d 641 (Mo. App. 1998)

Missouri Court of Appeals · 1998-07-22 · No. 22082 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals opinion applying or construing § 511.620 RSMo (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Id. Rule 74.11 and Section 511.620, provide a remedy when a judgment creditor fails to acknowledge satisfaction of judgment, but neither appear to be an exclusive remedy nor to preclude the award of damages if-satisfaction of record is not entered by the creditor “immediately” (Rule 74.11(c)), or “within a reasonable time after request.”
If a judgment creditor who has received satisfaction of a judgment fails to acknowledge such satisfaction immediately, any interested person may apply to the court where the judgment was entered for an order showing satisfaction. 511.620.
The issues are whether a debtor has a common-law action against a creditor whose judgment has been satisfied but who refuses to release it of record and, if so, whether Rule 74.11 or Section 511.620, RSMo 1994, has repealed or superseded such right.
