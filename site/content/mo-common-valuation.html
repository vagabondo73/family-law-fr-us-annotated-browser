Summary rule

“A trial court enjoys broad discretion in valuing marital property.” (Meier v. Meier, 2010). “A trial court has broad discretion in valuing marital property, but it cannot enter a valuation that is not supported by any evidence.” (Hayes v. Hayes, 2009). “A court values marital property as of the time of trial.” (Kratzer v. Kratzer, 2017); “Marital property is valued as of the date of trial.” (Seggelke v. Seggelke, 2010).

Kratzer v. Kratzer, 520 S.W.3d 809 (Mo. App. E.D. 2017)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2017-05-09 · No. ED 104462 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing § 452.335, § 452.330, § 452.355 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

A court values marital property as of the time of trial.
A court may award maintenance pursuant to § 452.335 only if the court finds a spouse: “(1) lacks sufficient property, including marital property apportioned to [her], to provide for [her] reasonable needs; and (2) is unable to support [herself] through appropriate employment^]” § 452.335.1.
Trial courts must divide marital property equitably after considering the factors laid out in § 452.330.1.
A court has the discretion to award reasonable attorney’s fees “after considering all relevant factors including the financial resources of both parties, the merits of the case and the actions of the parties during the pendency/ of the action[.]” Id. at 802; § 452.355.1.

Seggelke v. Seggelke, 319 S.W.3d 461 (Mo. App. E.D. 2010)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2010-08-17 · No. ED 92857 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing § 452.330 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

Marital property is valued as of the date of trial.
When dividing marital property, the trial court must consider the factors set out in section 452.330 and then divide the property in such proportions as it deems just.
Per section 452.330.1, the trial court must consider the following relevant factors: The economic circumstances of each spouse at the time the division of property is to become effective, including the desirability of awarding the family home or the right to live therein for reasonable periods to the spouse having custody of any children; (2) The contribution of each spouse to the acquisition of the marital property, including the contribution of a spouse as homemaker; (3) The value of the nonmarital property set apart to each spouse; (4) The conduct of the parties during the marriage ... “However, the trial court may attach the amount of weight to each of these it deems appropriate, and these factors are not exclusive.”
W.D.2007); rather, the distribution need only be fair and take into account the factors listed in section 452.330.1.

Meier v. Meier, 306 S.W.3d 692 (Mo. App. E.D. 2010)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District · 2010-03-23 · No. ED 92400 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Eastern District opinion applying or construing § 452.330 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

A trial court enjoys broad discretion in valuing marital property.
In Gustin, the court began its analysis with Section 452.330.1(1), which requires the trial court to divide marital property and marital debts in such proportions as the court deems just after considering all statutory factors, including the economic circumstances of the parties at the time the division of property is to become effective.
When dividing marital property and marital debts, a trial court is required under Section 452.330.1(1) and (3) to consider the economic circumstances of the parties at the time the division is to become effective as well as the value of non-marital property set apart to each spouse.
A party may meet this burden by showing that the property falls within one of the categories provided in Section 452.330.2, including that the spouse acquired the property by gift, bequest, devise, or descent.

Hayes v. Hayes, 288 S.W.3d 823 (Mo. App. S.D. 2009)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District · 2009-07-28 · No. SD 29208 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District opinion applying or construing § 452.330 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

A trial court has broad discretion in valuing marital property, but it cannot enter a valuation that is not supported by any evidence.
Silcox, 6 S.W.3d 899, 903 (Mo. banc 1999); see section 452.330.2.
In determining the two investment accounts to have been properly classified by the trial court as non-marital property, the court stated: Though child support payments received from a previous spouse are not an express exception in section 452.330, this list is not an exclusive list of exceptions.
Our legislature has not set forth child support payments as an express exception in section 452.330.
