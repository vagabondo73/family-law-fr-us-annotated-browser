452.365 — Party failing to comply with decree, effect of.

in forceIn force since : 1974-01-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=452.365 · Interactive browser

Current text

If a party fails to comply with a provision of a decree or temporary order or injunction, the obligation of the other party to make payments for support or maintenance or to permit visitation is not suspended but he may move the court to grant an appropriate order.

Miller v. Miller, 184 S.W.3d 174 (Mo. App. S.D. 2006)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District · 2006-02-21 · No. 26909 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Southern District opinion applying or construing § 452.375, § 452.365, § 452.400, § 452.330, § 452.355, 88.01 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

The General Assembly of Missouri, via section 452.375.4, has declared it to be “the public policy of this state that frequent, continuing and meaningful contact with both parents” after dissolution of marriage “is in the best interest of the child.”
In a similar vein, section 452.365 provides that “[i]f a party fails to comply with a provision of a decree ..., the obligation of the other party to make payments for support ... or to permit visitation is not suspended but he [or she] may move the court to grant an appropriate order.”
For this reason alone, the requirements of section 452.400.1-2 have been met and a supervised visitation order was proper with regard to Hayley.
Section 452.330.1 mandates that in a dissolution of marriage case “the court shall ... divide the marital property and marital debts in such proportions as the court deems just after considering all relevant factors.”
