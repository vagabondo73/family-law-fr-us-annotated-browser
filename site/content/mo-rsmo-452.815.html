452.815 — Forwarding copies of decrees.

in forceIn force since : 2009-08-28 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=452.815 · Interactive browser

Current text

The clerk of the circuit court of this state, at the request of the court of another state or at the request of any person who is affected by or has a legitimate interest in a custody decree, may, upon payment therefor, certify and forward a copy of the decree to that court or person.

art. 1180-17 · Légifrance452.815partial—The article restricts copies to specified persons, gives the formerly minor adult access to decisions concerning him or her, and requires judicial authorization for extracts requested by persons with a legitimate interest. RSMo 452.815 permits certification and forwarding of a custody decree to affected persons or persons with a legitimate interest, but lacks these detailed restrictions.
art. 1210-10 · Légifrance452.835, 452.815partial—The article requires copies of prosecutorial case materials to be supplied to the French Central Authority and potentially forwarded to a foreign central authority or specified persons. RSMo 452.835 provides for preserving and forwarding certified custody records at the request of another state’s court or law-enforcement official, while RSMo 452.815 concerns forwarding custody decrees; neither covers the same central-authority procedure fully.
art. 1223-1 · Légifrance452.815partial—After judgment, the judge may authorize copies to the protected adult, a minor aged sixteen or over, or the person responsible for the measure upon a showing of legitimate interest. Missouri provides related access or copying mechanisms, but not this general post-judgment protective-proceeding procedure.
art. 1223-2 · Légifrance452.815, 452.395partial—Family-council deliberations and judicial decisions may be copied only by specified persons, while a person with a legitimate interest may obtain extracts with judicial authorization. Missouri permits an affected person or one with a legitimate interest to obtain a copy of a custody decree and also allows certain records to be kept secret.
