77.03 — Plaintiff may Sue as a Poor Person – When

in forceIn force since : 1981-01-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=200282 · Interactive browser

Current text

If the plaintiff is a poor person, the court may permit the plaintiff to commence and prosecute the civil action without making a cost deposit or furnishing security for costs.

State ex rel. Steinmeyer v. Coburn, 671 S.W.2d 366 (Mo. App. W.D. 1984)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 1984-05-09 · No. WD 35141 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing Rule 77.03, § 511.370 RSMo, Rule 78.05 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

He argues that statutes and case law vested the judge with authority to grant relator leave to file in forma pauperis if the judge found from the evidence that relator was a “poor person” within the meaning of Rule 77.03.
A plaintiff may also sue out a writ of scire facias to revive a judgment within ten years, § 511.370, and even in an action pending on appeal the trial court has continuing jurisdiction over that collateral matter for the preservation of the fruits of the ultimate judgment or the status in quo of the parties.
Viewing plaintiff’s motion for leave to appeal in forma pauperis as a separate action but a continuation of the original action, as it was, defendants were entitled to the benefit of Rule 78.05 dealing with after-trial motions.
