525.170 — Judgment by default, how rendered final — extent and effect of against garnishee.

in forceIn force since : 1939-08-28 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=525.170 · Interactive browser

Current text

Such judgment by default may be proceeded on to final judgment, in like manner as in case of defendants in other civil actions; but no final judgment shall be rendered against the garnishee until there shall be final judgment against the defendant, and in no case for a greater amount than the amount sworn to by the plaintiff, with interest and costs, or for a greater amount than the garnishee shall appear to be liable for to the defendant.

Thus, in determining whether Rule 90.08 mandates a plaintiff to file a motion to compel before filing a motion for default judgment, we must interpret Missouri Supreme Court Rules and state statutes governing garnishment proceedings.

Beatty v. Conner, 923 S.W.2d 455 (Mo. App. W.D. 1996)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 1996-04-16 · No. WD 51209 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing Rule 74.06, § 525.140 RSMo, § 525.170 RSMo (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Rule 74.06(c) requires that all motions must be brought within a reasonable time, but must be brought not more than a year after the judgment is entered if irregular, whereas reasonableness is the only standard if the judgment is void. Therefore, if this judgment is irregular, it is time-barred from being set aside, but if it is void, it may be set aside if the motion was filed within a reasonable time.
Section 525.140, RSMo 1994, states in part, “[i]n default of such answer [to interrogatories], the plaintiff may take judgment by default against him _” Section 525.170, RSMo 1994, goes on to say: [s]uch judgment by default may be proceeded on to final judgment, in like manner as in case of defendants in other civil actions; but no final judgment shall be rendered against the garnishee until there shall be final judgment against the defendant, and in no ease for a greater amount than the amount sworn to by the plaintiff, with interest and costs, or for a greater amount than the garnishee shall appear to be liable for to the defendant.
Appellants contend that the default judgment was merely irregular under Rule 74.06(b)(3) and not void. Our standard of review is limited to whether the record convincingly indicates an abuse of the broad discretion which is vested in the trial court when ruling on a Rule 74.06 motion.

Butler v. Physicians Planning Service Corp., 724 S.W.2d 334 (Mo. App. 1987)

Missouri Court of Appeals · 1987-02-10 · No. 51563 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals opinion applying or construing § 525.170 RSMo, § 525.200 RSMo (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Prior to revision there was a Rule 90.16 which was a counterpart to § 525.170 and provided that no final judgment be entered against a garnishee “for a greater amount than the garnishee shall appear to be liable for to the defendant.”
Section 525.200 and Rule 90.17 require the court to find that garnishee possesses property of defendant and its value.
The court failed to comply with the dictate of § 525.170 RSMo. 1978 that “no final judgment shall be rendered ... for a greater amount than the garnishee shall appear to be liable for to the defendant.”
