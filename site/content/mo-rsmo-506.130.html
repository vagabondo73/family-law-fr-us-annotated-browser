506.130 — Summons shall be signed by clerk — contents.

in forceIn force since : 1943-08-28 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=506.130 · Interactive browser

Current text

The summons shall be signed by the clerk and dated the day it is issued, be under the seal of the court, contain the name of the court and the names of the parties, be directed to the defendant, state the name and address of the plaintiff's attorney, if any, otherwise the plaintiff's address, and the time within which the place where the defendant is required to appear and defend as provided by law, and shall notify him that in case of his failure to do so judgment by default will be rendered against him for the relief demanded in the petition.

Crain v. Crain, 19 S.W.3d 170 (Mo. App. W.D. 2000)

Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District · 2000-05-31 · No. WD 57679 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Missouri Court of Appeals, Western District opinion applying or construing § 506.130 RSMo, Rule 54.02, Rule 55.25 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Moreover, § 506.130 and Rule 54.02 require the summoned party be advised of the time within which, not the time at which, the defendant must appear and defend as provided by law.
Rule 55.25 requires the filing of a responsive pleading to the petition within 30 days of service.
Thus, the interpretation of § 506.130 dealing with the contents of a summons was not necessary to the court’s analysis and holding.

Elliott v. Hogan, 315 S.W.2d 840 (Mo. App. 1958)

St. Louis Court of Appeals · 1958-09-02 · No. 30010 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : St. Louis Court of Appeals opinion applying or construing § 506.130 RSMo (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Penrose v. Killoren, 354 Mo. 22, 188 S.W.2d 1; Messick v. Grainger, 356 Mo. 1227, 205 S.W.2d 739, contestee argues that the purpose of a summons is to notify a defendant when and where to appear to answer the complaint filed against him; that in ordinary civil actions the statutes not only fix the time within which a defendant is to appear and answer, but § 506.130 RSMo 1949, V.A.M.S. and Supreme Court Form No. 1 require that the summons state when he is to appear; and that the lack of such notification in the notice of contest was so fatally defective that the court never acquired jurisdiction of his person.

The Code itself, in Sqc. 506.130 requires the address of the plaintiff in the summons if he does not have an attorney.
Since the case was dismissed without prejudice, plaintiff has the right under Sec. 516.230 to commence a new action within one year from the time of the judgment of dismissal. This section is “a saving clause to prevent the bar [of limitations] which otherwise would be applicable”.
