71.01 — Verdict of Jury – General or Special

in forceIn force since : 1987-07-01 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=199730 · Rule 71 — all subdivisions (courts.mo.gov) · Interactive browser

Current text

The verdict of a jury is either general or special. A general verdict is one by which the jury pronounces generally upon all or any of the issues, either in favor of the plaintiff or defendant, and includes a verdict wherein the jury returns a finding of the plaintiff's total damages and assesses percentages of fault. A special verdict is one by which the jury finds the facts only, leaving the judgment to the court.

#### Committee Note – 1959

This Rule is the same as Section 106 of the 1943 Code (Section 510.220, RSMo 1959).

(Amended October 16, 1986, effective July 1, 1987.)

See also : 510.220 (mo-rsmo)

Missouri Pacific Railroad v. Whitehead & Kales Co., 566 S.W.2d 466 (Mo. 1978)

Supreme Court of Missouri · 1978-04-28 · No. 59906 published
Basis (b): identical text — text-identical

Justification : The opinion (1978-04-28) predates the current version of Rule 71.01 (in force since 1987-07-01) but quotes the language it applies — “one by which the jury pronounces generally upon all or any of the issues,” — and that language appears verbatim (normalized for punctuation/case) in the current text; the interpreted language is therefore identical in wording and function.

Summary (non-official) : Supreme Court of Missouri opinion applying or construing § 507.080 RSMo, Rule 71.01 (neutral index summary; see verbatim excerpts).

Included therein was Missouri’s first third-party practice statute, § 20, later § 507.080, RSMo 1959, wherein a defendant was permitted to bring into the lawsuit a person not a party to the action who was liable to him or to the plaintiff for all or part of the plaintiff’s claim against him.
Rule 71.01 defines a general verdict as “one by which the jury pronounces generally upon all or any of the issues,” so the jury’s verdict could assess damages, in accordance with the instructions, and award indemnity in an amount proportionate to the relative fault of the parties.
The instant case arises from the attempt of Missouri Pacific, pursuant to rule 52.11 and § 507.080, RSMo 1969, to bring Whitehead & Kales Company, Inc., the manufacturer and installer of the auto rack, into the Sampson case as a third party defendant liable to Missouri Pacific for all or a part of the plaintiff’s claim against Missouri Pacific.
