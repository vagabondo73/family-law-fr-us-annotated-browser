maintenance-contractual-vs-decretal — Maintenance under separation agreements — decretal, contractual and non-modifiable maintenance

in forceIn force since : 2001-01-09 · Applicable : MO

Official source : https://www.courtlistener.com/c/S.W.3d/34/387/ · Leading source: Cates v. Cates, 819 S.W.2d 731 (Mo. banc 1991) · Leading source: Lueckenotte v. Lueckenotte, 34 S.W.3d 387 (Mo. banc 2001) · Interactive browser

Summary rule

“Contractual maintenance, on the other hand, arises where the “maintenance provisions were not to be incorporated into the decree.”” “Contractual maintenance is enforceable by a separate action for breach of contract.” Maintenance set forth in the decree but agreed to be non-modifiable is a “hybrid” that “is decretal for purposes of enforcement and contractual for purposes of modification.” (Cates v. Cates, Mo. banc 1991). “In other words, separation agreement decretal maintenance is modifiable, unless the parties specifically agree to preclude or limit modification.” (Lueckenotte v. Lueckenotte, Mo. banc 2001, applying § 452.325.6).

Rule sources : Cates v. Cates, 819 S.W.2d 731 (Mo. banc 1991), Lueckenotte v. Lueckenotte, 34 S.W.3d 387 (Mo. banc 2001)

Lueckenotte v. Lueckenotte, 34 S.W.3d 387 (Mo. banc 2001)

Supreme Court of Missouri · 2001-01-09 · No. SC 82868 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Supreme Court of Missouri opinion applying or construing § 452.325, § 454.520, § 452.370, § 452.355, § 452.300, § 452.415 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

The trial court should also determine whether to make the order of child support retroactive to the date of the filing of the motion to modify child support and personal service.
Bryson, 624 S.W.2d 92, 95 (Mo.App.1981); Sec. 4-52.325.6 (“[e]xcept for terms concerning the support, custody or visitation of children, the decree may expressly preclude or limit modification of terms set forth in the decree if the separation agreement so provides.”) In other words, separation agreement decretal maintenance is modifiable, unless the parties specifically agree to preclude or limit modification.
Incorporating the provisions of the Lueckenotte agreement into the decree was sufficient to “expressly preclude ... modification of terms set forth in the decree_” Sec. 452.325.6.
“Section 454.520.4’s provision that interest ‘shall automatically accrue and attach to the underlying support or maintenance judgment or order’ makes assessment of interest for unpaid child support mandatory.”

Cates v. Cates, 819 S.W.2d 731 (Mo. banc 1991)

Supreme Court of Missouri · 1991-11-19 · No. 73368 published
Basis (a): decided on or after the current version

Summary (non-official) : Supreme Court of Missouri opinion applying or construing § 452.335, § 452.325 RSMo (neutral index summary; see excerpts).

Contractual maintenance, on the other hand, arises where the “maintenance provisions were not to be incorporated into the decree.”
See Section 452.-325.4(1) (“If the separation agreement provides that its terms shall not be set forth in the decree.”) Contractual maintenance is enforceable by a separate action for breach of contract.
This hybrid is decretal for purposes of enforcement and contractual for purposes of modification.
The court said: It is evident that the obligation to pay future statutory maintenance term of § 452.370.2 is meant to be understood in terms of maintenance § 452.335.
